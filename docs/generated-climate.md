# Generated climate reference

Climate layers are available at the selected date while paused. `generateClimate` evaluates geographic rules directly; it does not run warmup steps or spend astronomical time. This initial state is deterministic and intended for map generation, not a calibrated climate prediction or a converged equilibrium. Play subsequently evolves temperature and conservative water inventories from it.

## Temperature
Annual insolation uses24 uniformly spaced orbital phases. Seasonal forcing uses the selected declination, with a heat-capacity-dependent annual lag `atan(ω C / B)` and amplitude `cos(lag)`, where `B=4 ε σ Tglobal³`. Three neighbor-averaging passes estimate nearby ocean exposure; coastal land blends toward ocean capacity. Heat redistribution blends local forcing with `S/4` by `D/(D+2)`. These are illustrative initialization closures, not a fitted Earth climatology.

For land fraction `l` and mean land altitude `h`, the altitude factor is `f=max(0.55,exp(−0.0065 h l/288.15))`. Generated temperature is the local greybody estimate times `f`. Its near-sea-level Earth-temperature slope approximates6.5K/km; the lower factor bound prevents extrapolating that lapse line to negative Kelvin. The transient uses outgoing radiation `ε σ (T/f)^4`, with the matching stability derivative and actual generated initial energy. This effective radiation adjustment preserves cooler highlands; it is not a vertical atmospheric model, and no atmosphere is inferred from planet density.

## Wind and initial water
At latitude relative to `0.4 × solar declination`, three30-degree bands provide easterly/westerly/easterly surface wind components and equatorward/poleward/equatorward meridional components. Speed tapers sinusoidally at band edges, north/south strength is35% of configured strength, and fully land cells reduce speed25%. Retrograde spin reverses the east component; slower-than-Earth rotation attenuates it by the ratio of angular speeds. Fast rotation does not add extra circulation cells. This is an Earth-inspired parameterization, not an outcome of solved pressure, momentum or Coriolis dynamics.

Initial rain uses a wet equatorial band, a drier subtropical band and a wetter middle-latitude band. It scales with absorbed solar energy/latent heat, ocean availability sampled up to8 cells upwind, and terrain ascent/descent using a bounded exponential factor. Geographic reach is grid-dependent; it is not a resolved orographic cloud model. Rain is capped at30mm/day. Initial soil, relative humidity and standing water derive from estimated wetness; no claim is made that they represent accumulated historical rainfall. These values establish a fresh water-budget baseline.

Initial evaporation and outflow are diagnostic estimates. Outflow evaluates the reservoir-routing function over its stable step, retaining the calculated rate but restoring all stores without advancing time. After Play, precipitation/evaporation/discharge are actual latest-step transfers from the existing conservative water model. Initial orographic rainfall is not imposed on later steps. Seasonal vector winds update at shared step midpoints; simultaneous edge advection and diffusion use a bound valid for both donor directions. Wind vectors are part of water checkpoints, so pausing restores the displayed wind along with the water/temperature state.

The wind layer samples cell-centered vectors and draws arrows on the sphere. Texture encoding is signed east/north with about0.79m/s precision; probes use full-precision values. The displayed texture's48×24 grid matches the runtime climate grid. Terrain documents still save terrain/settings only, so a fresh reference can be generated after load.

## Limits and references
Temperature remains daily mean; instantaneous day/night lighting is separate. Slow/synchronous-spin daily averaging remains unsupported. There is no resolved pressure circulation, vertical atmosphere, ocean currents, hourly temperature, cloud/ice feedback or latent-heat feedback. Initial fluxes are labelled estimates until integration begins.

Qualitative geographic dependencies follow [Met Office temperature factors](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/met-office-for-schools/other-content/other-resources/understanding-weather). Wind-belt organization and seasonal convergence are informed by [NOAA global circulation](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/global/global-atmospheric-circulations) and [NOAA ITCZ](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/tropical/convergence-zone). The altitude reference uses the tropospheric lapse-rate convention in [NASA standard-atmosphere tables](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20050207438/downloads/20050207438.pdf). The formulas and caps above are our illustrative choices; none is presented as an implementation of an operational NOAA, Met Office or NASA model.
