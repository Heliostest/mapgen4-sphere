## Spec and validation findings

These are regional outcome failures at the changed closure, not claims that a single indicated line independently causes each failure. The P0 acceptance row in `../earth-status-reassessment-20261011/REPORT.md` explicitly requires India/southern Himalayan summer rain, foreland wetter than Tibet, Australian interior drier than its wet coasts and southwestern Australian winter rain. Conservation and decreasing time-step sensitivity are separate acceptance gates.

1. **[P1] India sea-to-land supply remains unresolved.** Location: `circulation.ts:41–46`; evidence: final record of `continuous-front-1800-continuous.json`. Matched GPCC-valid land precipitation increases from 102.63 to 240.58 mm, but the observational normal is 1138.06 mm. June–August model rain is 137.88 mm against 772.51 mm. On the explicitly separate whole-region ledger, net vapor transport remains an export of 243.23 mm, compared with 251.44 mm before; precipitation is 223.03 mm, evaporation 465.43 mm and vapor storage changes by −0.827 mm. The new potential therefore has not repaired the requested regional moisture supply. Reference initialization likewise gives India P approximately equal to E at every tested step. A completion claim must remain partial, or this supply defect needs another general closure change and renewed integration.

2. **[P1] The repaired mountain rain sink reverses the required foreland/plateau ordering.** Location: `water.ts:345–359`; same annual evidence. Foreland rain is 954.20 mm against GPCC 1307.78, while Tibet receives 1877.29 mm against 257.39. Tibet is almost twice as wet as the foreland rather than substantially drier. Reference initialization reproduces the same ordering at 1800/900/450 seconds (approximately 2146/2155/2156 mm in Tibet and 1361/1358/1356 mm at the Himalayan probe). This is not removed by smaller time steps. The new tests establish the temperature-reservoir semantics, but do not check mountain rain-shadow or regional acceptance.

3. **[P1] Australia regresses in both dry-interior ordering and southwestern seasonality.** Location: `water.ts:345–351`, together with the changed circulation potential. Interior annual rain rises from 647.15 to 1142.80 mm against GPCC 276.03, exceeding the eastern region's 780.75 mm. Southwestern DJF/JJA rain changes from 71.19/224.06 mm to 369.64/193.01 mm; GPCC is 51.30/226.67 mm. The patch reverses the previously correct winter-wet ordering. Reporting only an improved eastern annual total would hide these explicit P0 failures. Add outcome assertions or an explicit regional acceptance table before treating the closure as accepted.

The final 450-second run confirms all three findings: India 242.06 mm with whole-region vapor export −244.46 mm; foreland 989.14 mm versus Tibet 1893.43 mm; Australian interior 1144.91 mm versus east 785.51 mm; southwest DJF 373.78 mm versus JJA 192.77 mm. ## Follow-up diagnosis hypotheses

These were suggestions for isolated experiments, not validated repairs. Subsequent pressure/meridional/energy-center/orographic ablations are preserved separately, and the implementing agent rejected them because of regional regressions. None was added to product code; the suggestions below remain hypotheses rather than accepted remedies:

- `climate.ts:18` restricts the template convergence center to 0.4 × solar declination. At India the prescribed lower-branch meridional flow therefore keeps pointing south even in summer, and the new density perturbation has to overwhelm it. Deriving a transport-mode convergence center from the broadly smoothed thermal/energy state, with finite response time, would address the structural conflict more directly than multiplying the pressure-potential coefficient. Keep the legacy default separate and first test synthetic warmed continents, land/ocean swaps and seasonal reversal.
- `water.ts:358` subtracts explicit upslope cooling from the receiver's current air temperature. That temperature already contains the authored altitude factor through the transient thermal radiation law. Moving saturation from warm ground to colder air can therefore expose duplicated terrain cooling. Compare an isothermal synthetic slope with a slope whose air temperature already falls by the authored lapse; investigate transported donor-parcel temperature or the unresolved ascent cooling remaining after the represented lapse. The current mountain test uses uniform air temperature and cannot detect this accounting problem.
- The absolute warm-surface convective gate remains a separate closure problem: it can create large southwestern Australian summer rain even after the monsoon supply direction is corrected. Isolate convection, fronts and orographic contributions before changing their coefficients together.
