# Terrain water routing

Stage 2, following complete persistence (a96cb66): add an optional terrain-water mode while preserving Original map and existing coarse routing. Water is routed among the authored mesh's triangle reservoirs, with physical areas, bed elevations and edge sills. Each directed transfer has one donor and receiver or an ocean outlet; donor-limited transfers retain nonnegative stores. Closed depressions keep water until their head reaches a sill. This is a head-based reservoir model, not the Saint-Venant momentum equations or calibrated flood prediction.

The coarse climate continues to provide rain, snowmelt, evaporation and soil drainage. Before fine routing, differences in each climate cell's surface store are distributed to its fine reservoirs (proportional extraction for evaporation). Actual fine transfers update the coarse ledger using the same volume, so fine water is a partition of surface storage rather than an additional inventory. Coarse cells with no resolved land triangle retain their water and are reported as unresolved. Fine states and fluxes join visible rollback and complete saves; older complete files retain the coarse mode.

The natural-surface view uses fine discharge for river widths and standing depth for lake/wetland masks, drawn on the same geographic atlas as terrain. Rivers follow connected terrain edges; lake shore masks use actual interpolated terrain elevations. These are a geographic visualization of finite-volume reservoirs, not a surveyed inundation map; within-triangle shoreline volume is approximate. Original artwork remains selectable. Inspector distinguishes fine flow/depth from coarse climate values.

Acceptance: donor positivity and mass closure; empty/dry/ocean/closed basin/spill and changing source cases; fine/coarse inventory agreement; deterministic save/reload/visible rollback; actual browser wet/dry/time changes and terrain picking; visually inspect river junctions, lakes, coast, seams/poles and original-mode restoration. Complete tests/build/review and local commit before glacier work.

References: USGS [PRMS stream/lake routing and depression storage](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/tm6B8) motivates explicit storage/spill thresholds. [Audusse et al.](https://publications.imp.fu-berlin.de/478/) describes stronger positivity and still-water requirements for a shallow-water solver; this stage does not claim to implement that solver.
